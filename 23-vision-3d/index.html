How to read this CNN block

1 · Convolution: detect a pattern

Slide a 3 × 3 kernel across the 8 × 8 input. At each position, multiply the nine matching values and add them. With stride 1 and no padding, this produces the left-hand 6 × 6 map. Positive and negative responses indicate different matches to the chosen kernel.

2 · ReLU: keep positive responses

The middle map applies max(0, x) to every value: −0.8 becomes 0; +0.8 stays +0.8. Its size remains 6 × 6. This nonlinear step lets stacked layers represent more than a single linear filter.

3 · Max pooling: reduce resolution

Split the ReLU map into non-overlapping 2 × 2 regions. Keep only the largest value in each region (stride 2). The right-hand map is 3 × 3: 36 values become 9. Strong local responses survive, but their exact positions within each region are lost.

Try it: Change the input image or kernel. Follow the yellow boxes from left to right; the number panel lists the highlighted four values before and after ReLU and their maximum. Drag “Reveal” to separate the stages in 3D. The slider changes the view, not the calculation.

This is one illustrative block with hand-set weights, not a trained classifier. Pooling is a design choice—not a requirement of every CNN.
