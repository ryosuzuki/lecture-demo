A small model, with an inspectable contract.

A trained decoder, not a prerecorded pattern of changing bars. Equally importantly: not GPT, not a general English model, and not proof of broad semantic understanding.

Architecture

word ID → learned token embedding + learned position embedding

Repeat twice:
z = LayerNorm(x)
qₕ = W_Qₕ z; kₕ = W_Kₕ z; vₕ = W_Vₕ z
Aₕ = softmax(Qₕ Kₕᵀ / √2 + causal mask)
r = x + W_O concat(A₁V₁, A₂V₂)
x′ = r + W₂ ReLU(W₁ LayerNorm(r) + b₁) + b₂

logits = W_readout LayerNorm(x_final)
p = softmax(logits / temperature)
Parameters1,296; all initialized and trained from scratch
Vocabulary21 tokens, including a padding token
Blocks / heads2 decoder blocks, 2 heads per block
Residual / MLP dimensions8 / 16
Q, K, V dimension per headExactly 2, not a projection for visualization
NormalizationPre-norm, ε = 10⁻⁵; final LayerNorm before readout
Position informationLearned absolute position embeddings, added before block 1
ActivationReLU, not the GELU commonly used in GPT-2
TrainingCPU PyTorch; seed 23; AdamW; lr 0.012; weight decay 0.005; gradient norm clipped at 1; 1,200 full-batch steps
TokenizerWord-level whitespace tokenizer for this corpus, not BPE

PyTorch matrices are stored as [output × input]. The JavaScript implementation follows that convention. Q/K/V and attention output projections have no bias. MLPs and LayerNorms do have learned biases.

Original synthetic corpus

96 five-token sentences are generated from four determiners, eight context terms, and three equally frequent next verbs in the appropriate category:

the / a / this / that
× river / stream / creek / canal → bank → erodes / crumbles / slopes → .
× savings / central / commercial / investment → bank → lends / invests / profits → .

81 sentences (27 unique prefixes) are training data. 15 sentences (5 unique prefixes) are held out. The split holds out entire determiner–context pairs using (determinerIndex × 3 + contextIndex) % 7 == 0. No private documents, lecture transcripts, student data, or pretrained weights enter the corpus.

The loss predicts the next word at all four input positions: context after determiner, bank after context, verb after bank, and period after verb. The training sequence is five tokens long; the inference implementation allows up to eight tokens, but positions beyond the trained span should not be assumed useful.

Evaluation: say precisely what improved
StepTrain lossHeld-out total lossHeld-out bank→verb lossCategory accuracy (only 5 prefixes)
The growing held-out total loss is concentrated in the context prediction after a determiner: those exact pairs were excluded from training. It does not mean bank disambiguation worsened. At step 1,200, held-out losses by target are approximately:
context: 8.530 · bank: 0.000156 · verb: 1.09887 · period: 0.000119
For three equiprobable correct verbs, the best verb loss is ln(3) ≈ 1.099. Category accuracy and total next-token loss answer different questions. Five held-out prefixes provide only a tiny controlled check—not an estimate of real-world language competence. The default final checkpoint is shown for demonstration, not because it minimizes total validation loss.


What the controls really do
  • Context switch: replaces one token in the input, clears interventions, and reruns the same learned model.
  • Q drag: overrides one receiving position’s query in the selected head and block. Learned weights are unchanged.
  • V drag: overrides one source value for every receiving query in that head. Its Q/K attention weights remain fixed; downstream blocks can change.
  • Head gain: scales the selected head’s message before concatenation and W_O. Zero removes that head’s message, not the other head or the residual path.
  • Mask OFF: permits future-position attention. This is an intervention outside the causal training setup.
  • Checkpoint: loads weights from one real saved training step. The browser is not training on each slider movement.
  • Temperature: changes only the final vocabulary softmax.
Attention weights are not a complete explanation of an output. Their meaning depends on the values, projections, other heads and later layers. The learned coordinate axes are not labeled “money” or “river.” No head was assigned a grammatical or semantic role by the author.


Verification and reproduction
A separate JavaScript implementation is checked against the PyTorch forward pass at every intermediate activation on five prompts. Maximum absolute error: 2.7 × 10⁻⁶. Browser tests cover Q and V dragging, unchanged selected-head attention for V-only intervention, context-only comparison reset, readout selection, causal invariance on future append, mask toggle, checkpoint keyboard control, and all seven chapters at 390px mobile width.

Relation to 3Blue1Brown and GPT
The design follows the conceptual sequence in 3Blue1Brown’s Attention lesson: motivate contextual updates, geometrically compare Q/K, route values, then connect to the residual stream. All graphics and code here are original. This model has far fewer dimensions, layers and tokens than GPT; its 2-D heads are chosen to make the actual geometry drawable.
After the controlled lesson, use Transformer Explainer to inspect GPT-2. Its page and examples were checked; an arbitrary-input generation after completing its model download is not part of this prototype’s verification.
